1. USDA food-data fallback

When Open Food Facts cannot find a product or generic food, DiabEats now searches USDA FoodData Central.

This supports foods without ordinary barcodes, such as:

Yellow onion
Kiwi
Apples
Generic produce
Other basic foods

Important behavior:

Verified USDA nutrition data is used.
Missing nutrients remain unknown—they are not changed to zero.
DiabEats does not invent nutrition values.
The result identifies USDA FoodData Central as the source.
A USDA source link appears on the result page.

New configuration required:

USDA_FDC_API_KEY

Relevant files:

server/services/usda-fooddata-central.ts
tests/usda-fooddata-central.test.ts
app/(tabs)/biotrace.tsx
components/BioTraceResult.tsx
server/routes.ts
shared/biotrace.ts
2. BioTrace scanning was stabilized

The scanner now distinguishes among:

UPC/EAN/GTIN barcodes
Open Food Facts product URLs
Product or produce names
DiabEats test-menu QR codes
Unsupported or malformed data

Important safety behavior:

Exact barcode → exact product lookup.
Food name → product search, not a guessed product.
Unknown result → clearly reported as unknown.
Test-menu UI opens only for the explicit schema:
diabeats.test.menu.v1
Random JSON, malformed URLs, and unsupported QR content should not be mistaken for products or menus.
3. Offline/cached barcode fallback

If Open Food Facts is temporarily unavailable, DiabEats can use a previously cached verified product.

The response is marked internally as:

cached: true
stale: true

This makes scanning more reliable without manufacturing new data.

4. Local scan history improvements
Local BioTrace history can now be retrieved and displayed.
Rapid duplicate scans of the same barcode within approximately 30 seconds are removed.
Pending offline scans remain available for later synchronization.
Saved/history behavior was stabilized.
5. Onboarding simplified

Onboarding was reduced from five steps to four.

The preset carbohydrate-target step was removed because DiabEats should not assign a personal medical target. The new wording tells users to add a carbohydrate target only when it comes from their care plan.

The final message now describes the app as providing educational food insights—not medical treatment or prediction.

6. Safer result language

The displayed classifications changed:

Previous wording	Current wording
Better Fit	Better choice
Use with Caution	Moderate impact
Limit	Higher impact
Insufficient Information	Not enough information

The app should continue presenting meal patterns and food-impact guidance, not claiming to predict an exact glucose increase.

7. Restaurant test data cleanup

The restaurant data was reorganized into defined types and exports.

Important clarification:

DiabEats does not automatically import restaurants.
Missing restaurants or menus are submitted by users.
Test restaurants such as “The Green Fork” are not proof of automatic importing.
Automatic restaurant discovery remains a future idea, not a completed feature.
8. Android permission/compliance protection

The Android version should:

Use the system photo picker.
Avoid READ_MEDIA_IMAGES.
Avoid READ_MEDIA_VIDEO.
Request camera permission only when camera scanning is used.

This was important because Google Play previously rejected the app over media permissions.
